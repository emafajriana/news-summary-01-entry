World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. Russia and Ukraine, in their first direct talks in nine months, agreed in principle in Abu Dhabi on the war's largest prisoner exchange — over 1,000 people — but no breakthrough on territory (Reuters). Attacks in the Strait of Hormuz continued — a tanker was struck off Oman, Iran's Guards said two more hit naval mines — after 37 ships were attacked in October, a record month (Reuters). Nvidia lost some $430 billion in market value in two sessions as its board opened an inquiry into reports CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation; Baird cut its target to $200 from $500 (Reuters). US inflation beat forecasts in October — prices rose 0.4%, core 0.3% for a third straight month — and with the Fed signalling a December hike, the two-year yield jumped 19 basis points to 4.75% (Reuters). Leaders of 102 countries opened a two-day summit at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey (AFP). US prosecutors charged three men over nearly 20 alleged Iran-backed plots, including one against RAF Fairford in England; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved onto a military base after threats (New York Times). British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army soldier with a NATO unit in England on suspicion of spying for Russia (AP). The US House voted 211-204 to refer impeachment articles against Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing the push (Reuters). Israel's new parliament was sworn in without a government after elections cost Benjamin Netanyahu his majority, leaving Gadi Eisenkot to try to form one (BBC). DR Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths — second-largest on record; the Bundibugyo strain has no vaccine or treatment (Reuters). Anthropic's bankers weighed pricing its IPO above the $70-78 range on heavy demand, lifting the target valuation to $1.5 trillion or more (FT). Salvadoran forces destroyed the 400 Mawozo gang's headquarters in Haiti, President Nayib Bukele said (Al Jazeera).
