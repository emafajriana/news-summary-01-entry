World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. The US government shutdown ended after 43 days as the House passed the bill 222-209 and Trump signed it, funding agencies to 30 January; health-insurance subsidies expiring in December stay unresolved (AP). DR Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths, the second-largest ever; Bundibugyo has no vaccine or treatment. Kenya discharged its last patient, starting the 42-day Ebola-free countdown (Reuters). At their first talks in nine months in Abu Dhabi, Russia and Ukraine agreed to the war's largest prisoner swap—over 1,000—with no territorial breakthrough (Reuters). Rubio told the G7 US pressure made major combat unnecessary (New York Times). Tehran kept Hormuz shut pending seven conditions; a tanker was hit off Oman; the Guards said two more hit mines after October's record 37 attacks (Reuters). Unions said four million joined Spain's first general strike in 14 years over housing, three weeks before a snap election (Reuters). Prices rose 0.4%, core 0.3%; 2-year yields rose 19bp to 4.75%, while Fed Governor Waller backed a December hike (Reuters). A 211-204 House vote sent Pete Hegseth's impeachment articles to committee (Reuters). Judge Jeffrey Kuntz let Trump's $10 billion BBC suit proceed, dismissing the unfair-trade-practices claim; the BBC admitted its Panorama edit of January 6, 2021 was misleading—it implied Trump urged the Capitol attack. It apologized but denies defamation (Reuters). UN chief António Guterres told 102 leaders at COP31 in Antalya: inaction risks mutually assured destruction (Reuters).
